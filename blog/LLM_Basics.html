Foundations

Large Language Models (LLMs)

An LLM is a highly advanced computer system trained to look at a sequence of words and predict the absolute most logical next word.

  • Tokenization: Slicing text into smaller chunks called "tokens" (words or pieces of words) and turning them into numbers.
  • Parameters: The internal settings or "brain cells" of the AI that it fine-tunes during training to learn what words mean.
  • Inference: The actual processing loop where you give the AI a prompt and it calculates the answer.
Simple Analogy Think of a super-advanced version of your phone's predictive text. Instead of just guessing the next word based on the last thing you typed, it reads thousands of books, articles, and websites to figure out exactly what word should follow your sentence.

The Core Engine

The Transformer Core Architecture

The Transformer is the specific framework that makes modern AI possible. Its secret superpower is a mechanism called Self-Attention.

  • Parallel Processing: Older systems read sentences word-by-word. Transformers look at an entire sentence or page all at once.
  • Self-Attention: The AI calculates mathematical links between all words in a sentence at the same time to understand the true context.
Simple Analogy When you read the word "bank" in the sentence "The bank was muddy from the river," your brain instantly connects "bank" to "river" instead of a money bank. Self-attention allows the AI to highlight and connect related words across a whole page instantly.

Retrieval Networks

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)

An AI's internal knowledge is locked at the moment it finishes training. If you ask it about a recent event or a private file, it might make things up (called a hallucination). RAG solves this.

  • User Prompt: You ask the AI a question.
  • Search & Match: Before answering, a system searches a private library or database for matching data.
  • Augment & Generate: The system pastes those facts into your prompt background so the AI gives a fully accurate verified answer.
Simple Analogy Instead of expecting a lawyer to memorize every single local corporate law book ever written (Standard LLM), you give them a custom search engine to pull up the exact rule files needed right before they write your legal document (RAG).

Interface Standardization

Model Context Protocol (MCP)

MCP is an open standard that acts as a secure, universal bridge allowing AI models to safely read data from databases, local files, and computer applications.

  • Universal Connection: Uses a single setup format so developers do not have to write custom code every time they want to link an AI to a new program.
  • Safety Isolation: It stops an AI agent from accidentally wiping out a computer's entire file system.
Simple Analogy Think of MCP as a universal USB port for AI. Instead of engineering a different custom cable to attach an AI to a private database, an email app, or a computer calendar, MCP provides a standard plug that lets the AI connect to anything safely.
